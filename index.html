Intelligent Systems Engineering · Indiana University

I’m Curtez, an engineer learning to build intelligent systems people can trust.

I study Intelligent Systems Engineering (CS track) with a minor in Business at Indiana University Bloomington’s Luddy School, where I’m a Hudson & Holland Scholar. I like understanding a system all the way down, from logic gates to machine-learning models, and I’m working toward a career in AI/ML product development.

Follow my path Résumé (PDF)

What I’m working toward

AI that helps students

I want to apply AI to help create sustainable solutions and tools for students with regards to problems in academics and well-being, and to help peers build real fluency with AI tools.

How computers really work

Building a CPU from the gate level up made me want to understand every layer a program runs on, not just the top one.

Engineering that earns trust

The systems I want to build make decisions about people. I want to be the engineer who asks who carries the risk when they get it wrong.

The path so far

From logic gates to learning machines

Each project taught me something the next one needed.

  1. Foundations

    A computer from NAND gates up

    Working through Nand2Tetris showed me how every layer of a computer depends on the one below it, from logic gates to a working CPU in HDL.

  2. Intro Programming · E111

    Ocean Cleanup Crew

    My first program for a real audience: a C game that teaches kids ages 7–12 about protecting the ocean.

  3. Computer Architecture · E110

    Hack32

    I created the architecture for our team’s 32-bit reimagining of the Nand2Tetris Hack computer and made the slides we presented. Our professor asked to share it with future classes.

  4. Personal project

    CourtVisionAI

    Full-stack machine learning that predicts NBA game outcomes and player props, with a Flask model server, a Next.js front end, and live data.

  5. Now · Fall 2026

    Intelligent Systems I + Engineering Ethics

    Training classifiers in E221 while E299 asks the harder question: who is responsible when an intelligent system fails?

  6. Next

    AI/ML product development

    Building intelligent systems that are useful, fair, and safe for the people who depend on them.
